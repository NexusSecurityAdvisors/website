Red Team & Penetration Testing 10

What offensive testing is, why it pays off, what regulations require it, and the references testers use.

ScanVulnerability scanning

Automated tools list known weaknesses. Broad and frequent, but they don't prove anything can be exploited.

PentestPenetration testing

Authorized experts try to exploit weaknesses in a defined scope (network, app, cloud) and show the real impact.

Red teamRed teaming

A goal-based, stealthy campaign that emulates a real adversary to test people, process, and technology, including whether your team notices.

PurplePurple teaming

Attackers and defenders work side by side, test by test, to tune detections and close gaps in real time.

Common use cases
  • External perimeter: what an internet attacker can reach today
  • Internal network and Active Directory: how far a phished laptop gets
  • Web apps and application programming interfaces (APIs): OWASP Top 10, auth, and business-logic flaws
  • Cloud: identity and access management (IAM) misconfiguration, exposed storage, and privilege escalation
  • AI and LLM applications: prompt injection, data leakage, and agent misuse
  • Social engineering: phishing, vishing, and help-desk pretexting
  • Physical and wireless: badge access, tailgating, and rogue Wi-Fi
  • Before a launch, merger, or audit: independent proof before it counts
Why request these services: the business benefits
  • Proof, not theory: shows which risks are actually exploitable so you fix what matters first
  • Smarter spending: validates whether existing tools and controls work before you buy more
  • Tests your response: measures how fast your team or provider detects and contains an attack
  • Evidence for others: reports satisfy auditors, customers' security questionnaires, and insurers
  • Board-ready risk: turns technical findings into business impact leaders can act on
  • Cheaper than a breach: finding a path in yourself costs far less than an incident
When it's required for compliance
PCI DSS v4.0.1Req. 11.4: internal and external penetration tests at least every 12 months and after significant changes, plus segmentation testing.
New York State Department of Financial Services (NYDFS) 23 New York Codes, Rules and Regulations (NYCRR) 500500.5: penetration testing at least annually, from inside and outside your system boundaries.
EU Digital Operational Resilience Act (DORA)Art. 26: threat-led penetration testing (TLPT) at least every three years for financial entities designated by authorities.
FedRAMPPenetration testing as part of initial and annual assessments.
CMMC 2.0Level 2 requires vulnerability scanning and periodic control assessments (NIST SP 800-171). Level 3 adds penetration testing under NIST SP 800-172.
HIPAAThe Security Rule requires periodic technical evaluations. The proposed 2025 update would require penetration testing at least every 12 months.
System and Organization Controls (SOC) 2Not explicitly mandated, but auditors and customers commonly expect an annual pentest as evidence of monitoring controls.
ISO/IEC 27001:2022Annex A 8.8 (technical vulnerabilities) and 8.29 (security testing) are commonly met with pentesting.
EU Cyber Resilience ActAnnex I requires manufacturers to run effective and regular security tests and reviews of products with digital elements.
Cyber insuranceUnderwriters increasingly ask for recent pentest results and remediation evidence.

Summary for awareness, not legal advice. Confirm requirements against the current official text for your situation.

Before you hire a tester
  • Define scope, goals, and what's off-limits in writing
  • Get signed authorization from the system owner, including any cloud or hosting providers' rules
  • Agree on rules of engagement, testing windows, and an emergency stop contact
  • Ask for sample reports, tester qualifications, and accreditation (such as CREST)
  • Require secure handling and deletion of any data the testers access
  • Budget for remediation and a retest to prove fixes work

    Phishing, Smishing & Email Threats 10

    Recognize, prevent, and report phishing, text-message scams, and business email compromise.

    Spot it
    • Urgency, threats, or “act now” pressure
    • Sender or link that doesn't match the real domain
    • Unexpected attachments, QR codes, or login pages
    • Texts about packages, tolls, or bank holds (smishing)
    Stop it
    • Phishing-resistant MFA (passkeys / FIDO2) for email and admins
    • Sender Policy Framework (SPF), DomainKeys Identified Mail (DKIM), and Domain-based Message Authentication, Reporting, and Conformance (DMARC) at enforcement on your domain
    • Email filtering, link rewriting, and attachment sandboxing
    • Verify payment or bank-detail changes by phone, never by reply
    Report it
    • Use your email's “Report phishing” button and tell information technology (IT)
    • Forward scam texts to 7726 (SPAM) in the U.S.
    • Report fraud or wire loss to the Federal Bureau of Investigation (FBI) IC3 immediately
    • If you clicked: change passwords, then call IT right away
