How It Works

Five steps to a useful tabletop exercise.

1. Plan

Pick a scenario that matches your top risks, set two to four objectives, and invite the decision-makers. Allow 90 to 120 minutes.

2. Brief

Read the ground rules: no fault, no gotchas, and answer with the resources you really have today.

3. Play

Read each inject, then discuss. Push for specifics: who decides, how fast, and with what information.

4. Debrief (“hotwash”)

Capture what went well, what was unclear, and three to five action items with owners and due dates. The scribe reads back the exercise log.

5. Document and improve

File the exercise log, complete the after-action report, update your incident response plan, and schedule the next exercise. Aim for at least one per year.

Roles

Who should be in the room.

In a small business one person often holds several roles. Assign each one before the exercise and name a backup for every decision-maker. The kit includes the questions each role should be ready to answer.

RoleResponsibilities during an incident
FacilitatorRuns the exercise. Keeps time, reads injects, asks follow-up questions, and keeps the discussion blame-free.
Scribe / ObserverRecords decisions, gaps, assumptions, and action items. Does not participate in the discussion.
Executive Sponsor (CEO / Owner)Sets priorities, approves major decisions (shutting down systems, paying, notifying), and speaks for the company.
Incident CommanderCoordinates the response, assigns tasks, tracks status, and runs regular update calls. Often the virtual chief information security officer (vCISO) or IT/security lead.
information technology (IT) Lead / Managed Service Provider (MSP)Investigates, contains, and restores systems. Preserves evidence and logs.
Security Lead / vCISOAssesses scope and severity, guides containment and forensics, and maps actions to the incident response plan.
Legal / Privacy CounselAdvises on privilege, regulatory and contractual notification duties, law enforcement contact, and payment legality.
Communications / Public RelationsPrepares internal, customer, partner, media, and regulator messaging. Controls a single source of truth.
Finance / chief financial officer (CFO)Manages payments, banking contacts, insurance claims, and emergency spending authority.
Human ResourcesHandles employee communications, insider issues, payroll continuity, and staff well-being during a long incident.
Operations / Business ContinuityKeeps the business serving customers using manual workarounds while systems are restored.
Cyber Insurance / Broker LiaisonNotifies the carrier promptly and coordinates approved vendors (breach coach, forensics, negotiators).
Document It

Keep an exercise log, so the next exercise starts where this one ended.

The most valuable output of a tabletop is the written record. Name a scribe before you start, log every event and decision as it happens, and file the log and after-action report with your incident response plan. Insurers, auditors, and customers increasingly ask for this evidence.

What to record

  • Date, scenario, participants, and their roles
  • Each event (inject), the time it was introduced, and what the team decided
  • Who owned each decision and how long it took
  • Missing information, contacts, tools, or authority
  • Action items with an owner and a due date

How to use it next time

  • Open the next exercise by reviewing last time's action items
  • Re-test the gaps you found, or pick a harder scenario
  • Track improvement: faster decisions, fewer unknowns
  • Update your incident response plan and contact list
  • Keep logs for at least the life of your insurance policy and contracts

Any Size

Scale the roles to your company.

You do not need twelve people. Every scenario works with combined roles, as long as you name a facilitator, a scribe, and one decision-maker.

Company sizeWho should attendHow roles combine
Micro (1 to 10 people)Owner, office manager, IT provider; insurance broker or attorney by phoneOwner leads and decides; IT provider covers technology and security; a trusted outsider facilitates; the office manager keeps the log.
Small (10 to 50 people)Owner or CEO, operations lead, finance, IT lead or managed service provider, HR or office managerOperations lead runs the response; finance also handles insurance; HR also handles communications.
Mid-sized (50 to 500 people)Executive team, IT and security leads, legal, communications, HR, finance, operationsAssign each role to one person with a named backup, and consider a separate technical tabletop for the IT team.
Scenarios

Eight ready-to-run scenarios.

An inject is a new piece of the story the facilitator reads aloud, such as “the bank calls” or “a reporter emails.” The hotwash is the short debrief at the end. Each scenario includes objectives, recommended participants, timed injects, discussion questions by role, key decisions to look for, and the related National Institute of Standards and Technology (NIST) Cybersecurity Framework (CSF) 2.0 functions.
