Phishing in 60 seconds

Phishing is a message that tricks you into clicking, paying, or sharing a password. Red flags: urgency, secrecy, unexpected attachments, and links whose real domain does not match the sender. When in doubt, contact the person or company another way. Report it; do not just delete it.

Passwords in 60 seconds

Use long passphrases of four or more random words, a unique one for every account, stored in a password manager. Turn on multi-factor authentication (MFA), ideally passkeys. Change a password when there is a sign it leaked, not on a fixed schedule.

Ransomware in 60 seconds

Ransomware locks your files and often steals copies to pressure you into paying. Your best protection is MFA, fast patching, and backups kept offline and tested. If it happens, disconnect affected devices, call your information technology (IT) provider and insurer, and do not pay without legal advice.

Deepfakes in 60 seconds

artificial intelligence (AI) can now fake a voice from a short clip and a face on a live video call. You cannot rely on spotting it. Instead, verify any request for money or access by calling back on a known number, and require two people to approve payments.

Safe AI use in 60 seconds

Use approved business AI tools, never paste confidential or personal data into free chatbots, and check every fact, figure, and citation the AI gives you. AI can be confidently wrong. A person stays responsible for the final decision.

Updates in 60 seconds

Most attacks use flaws that already have fixes. Turn on automatic updates for phones, computers, browsers, and apps, and replace devices that no longer get updates. Routers, firewalls, and virtual private networks (VPNs) need updates too.

Capture the Flag of the Month · November 2026

Decode the hidden message.

A capture the flag (CTF) is a security puzzle where you find a hidden “flag.” This month's beginner challenge takes two steps in the free CyberChef tool.

The challenge

We intercepted this message:

QVJLSEZ7ZnJlaXJfc3ZlZmdfZnJwaGVyX255am5sZn0=
  • Hint 1: The ending “=” is a clue about how it is encoded.
  • Hint 2: After the first step, the letters are shifted. Think Julius Caesar, 13 places.
  • Format: NEXUS{...}
