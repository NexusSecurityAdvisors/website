Our approach: tools find the issues, people close them.

How we assess, prioritize, execute, and prove progress, and an interactive Risk Lab that shows how findings turn into decisions.

Our Foundation

Servant leadership, with people at the center of security.

Security tools, tactics, techniques, and procedures (TTPs) are necessary, but they are never enough. Every serious breach we study has a human moment: a rushed approval, an unasked question, an alert nobody owned. That is why we lead by serving your people first, and why we keep a trained, accountable human in the loop for every decision that matters, including decisions made with the help of artificial intelligence (AI).

01

We serve, then lead

We listen to your team before we recommend anything, explain the “why” in plain English, and measure our success by how capable and confident your people become, not by how dependent they are on us.

02

Humans in the loop

Technology finds and flags; people decide. We design processes so a named person reviews and owns high-impact actions, from approving payments to acting on AI-generated advice.

03

Culture over checklists

Policies only work when people believe in them. We build security habits through coaching, tabletop exercises, and recognition, so staff report mistakes early instead of hiding them.

04

Quality work, honestly delivered

We tell you plainly what matters, what does not, and when you do not need us. Every engagement ends with clear ownership, documentation your team can use, and skills that stay with you.

Our Approach

Tools find the issues. People close them.

Every finding gets a plain-language title, a business impact, a named owner, a due date, and a retest date. Have the plan in place, then work the plan.

A typical first 90 days

  1. Weeks 1–2 · DiscoverStakeholder interviews, document review, asset and data inventory, quick-win list
  2. Weeks 3–4 · AssessCSF 2.0 current-state profile, control testing, risk register with 5×5 scoring
  3. Day 30 · ReportExecutive briefing: top risks, target profile, and a funded 30/60/90-day roadmap
  4. Days 31–90 · ExecuteOwners close high-priority gaps; policies, incident response (IR) plan, and vendor reviews put in place
  5. Day 90 · MeasureRetest, update the risk register, and deliver the first board-level scorecard

Timelines are illustrative and scale with organization size and scope.

How we measure progress

Measures are selected using National Institute of Standards and Technology (NIST) Special Publication (SP) 800-55 guidance and reported as trends, not one-off snapshots.

The Nexus CISO Framework: 12 pillars

Every engagement is organized around twelve pillars. Each one maps to the NIST CSF 2.0 functions and categories it supports, and, for organizations in scope, to the EU Network and Information Security Directive (NIS2) and the EU Cyber Resilience Act (CRA).

  1. Business-Aligned Risk Strategy

    Security priorities set by what the business cannot afford to lose, scored the same way every time.

    CSF 2.0
    GV.OC, GV.RM, ID.RA
    NIS2
    Art. 20; Art. 21(2)(a)
    CRA
    Art. 13 product risk assessment

  2. AI Security & Governance

    Inventory, risk-rate, and govern AI use and AI features, aligned to the NIST AI Risk Management Framework (AI RMF) and ISO/IEC 42001.

    CSF 2.0
    GV.RM, ID.AM, PR.DS
    NIS2
    Art. 21(2)(a), (e)
    CRA
    Annex I, for AI-enabled products

  3. Third-Party & Supply Chain Risk

    Know which vendors and components you depend on, what they can reach, and what happens if one fails.

    CSF 2.0
    GV.SC
    NIS2
    Art. 21(2)(d), (e)
    CRA
    Art. 13 component due diligence; Annex I Part II (software bill of materials)

  4. Incident Response & Business Continuity

    A tested plan, clear decision rights, backups that restore, and reporting clocks you can meet.

    CSF 2.0
    RS, RC
    NIS2
    Art. 21(2)(b), (c); Art. 23 reporting
    CRA
    Art. 14 reporting

  5. Compliance & Audit Readiness

    One control set mapped to many frameworks, with evidence collected as you go instead of the week before the audit.

    CSF 2.0
    GV.OC, GV.OV, ID.IM
    NIS2
    Art. 21(2)(f)
    CRA
    Conformity assessment and technical documentation

  6. Cloud Security Architecture

    Secure-by-default configurations, segmentation, encryption, and logging across your cloud and SaaS estate.

    CSF 2.0
    PR.PS, PR.IR, PR.DS
    NIS2
    Art. 21(2)(e), (h)
    CRA
    Annex I Part I (secure by default)

  7. Identity & Access Management

    MFA everywhere it matters, least privilege, and access reviews that remove what nobody needs.

    CSF 2.0
    PR.AA
    NIS2
    Art. 21(2)(i), (j)
    CRA
    Annex I Part I (unauthorized access)

  8. Security Culture & Awareness

    Training people actually use, measured by how often they report, not how often they click.

    CSF 2.0
    PR.AT, GV.RR
    NIS2
    Art. 20(2); Art. 21(2)(g)
    CRA
    Indirect

  9. Budget Optimization & ROI

    Spend tied to risk reduced, so every dollar has a reason the board can follow.

    CSF 2.0
    GV.RR, GV.OV
    NIS2
    Art. 20; Art. 21(1) proportionality
    CRA
    Indirect

  10. Emerging Threat Intelligence

    Track the threats and vulnerabilities that matter to your sector and act on them before they act on you.

    CSF 2.0
    ID.RA, DE.CM, DE.AE
    NIS2
    Art. 21(2)(e); Art. 29 information sharing
    CRA
    Annex I Part II (vulnerability handling); Art. 14

  11. Talent Development & Team Building

    Grow the people who run security day to day, with clear roles, coaching, and a bench that covers absences.

    CSF 2.0
    GV.RR, PR.AT
    NIS2
    Art. 20(2); Art. 21(2)(i)
    CRA
    Indirect

  12. Program Leadership & Continuous Improvement

    Servant leadership, Lean Six Sigma, Agile, and program management that turn the plan into closed findings and measurable gains.

    CSF 2.0
    GV.OV, ID.IM
    NIS2
    Art. 21(2)(f)
    CRA
    Indirect

CSF 2.0 codes: GV Govern, ID Identify, PR Protect, DE Detect, RS Respond, RC Recover. NIS2 articles refer to Directive (EU) 2022/2555; CRA references refer to Regulation (EU) 2024/2847, which applies to manufacturers of products with digital elements. A dash means the pillar supports the regulation indirectly.quot;IndirectA dash means the pillar supports the regulation indirectly.quot; means the pillar supports the regulation without mapping to a specific requirement. Mappings show where each pillar does most of its work. They are not a legal determination of which obligations apply to your organization.

Methodology foundations & references

Our methods draw on established, peer-reviewed and consensus standards, so results are repeatable and defensible to auditors, boards, and regulators:

  1. NIST, The NIST Cybersecurity Framework (CSF) 2.0, NIST CSWP 29, 2024. nist.gov
  2. NIST, Guide for Conducting Risk Assessments, SP 800-30 Rev. 1, 2012. csrc.nist.gov
  3. NIST, Risk Management Framework for Information Systems and Organizations, SP 800-37 Rev. 2, 2018. csrc.nist.gov
  4. NIST, Measurement Guide for Information Security, Vol. 1, SP 800-55 Vol. 1, 2024. csrc.nist.gov
  5. NIST, Incident Response Recommendations and Considerations for Cybersecurity Risk Management, SP 800-61 Rev. 3, 2025. csrc.nist.gov
  6. ISO, ISO 31000:2018 Risk management — Guidelines. iso.org
  7. International Organization for Standardization and International Electrotechnical Commission (ISO/IEC), ISO/IEC 27005:2022 Guidance on managing information security risks. iso.org
  8. The Open Group, Open Factor Analysis of Information Risk (FAIR)™ risk analysis, for quantifying risk in financial terms when needed. opengroup.org
  9. European Parliament and Council, Directive (EU) 2022/2555 on measures for a high common level of cybersecurity across the Union (NIS2 Directive), 2022. eur-lex.europa.eu
  10. European Parliament and Council, Regulation (EU) 2024/2847 on horizontal cybersecurity requirements for products with digital elements (Cyber Resilience Act), 2024. eur-lex.europa.eu
  11. NIST, Artificial Intelligence Risk Management Framework (AI RMF 1.0), NIST AI 100-1, 2023. nist.gov
  12. Center for Internet Security (CIS), CIS Critical Security Controls. cisecurity.org
