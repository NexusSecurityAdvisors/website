Government Contracting

Win and keep government work with security that holds up to scrutiny.

Federal, defense, and state and local contracts come with cybersecurity requirements written into the contract. Missing them can cost you an award, a renewal, or worse. Nexus Security Advisors, a U.S. Small Business Administration (SBA)-certified Service-Disabled Veteran-Owned Small Business (SDVOSB), helps small and mid-sized contractors understand what applies, close the gaps, and prove it.

Who It's For

Built for small and mid-sized government contractors.

Defense suppliers and subcontractors

Manufacturers, engineering firms, and service providers handling federal contract information or controlled unclassified information for the Department of Defense (DoD), now also called the Department of War, or its prime contractors.

Federal civilian contractors

Businesses supporting civilian agencies that must protect government data and answer security questionnaires.

State and local government vendors

Software and service providers selling to states, cities, school districts, and public utilities.

Cloud and software companies

Software-as-a-service (SaaS) providers exploring federal or state authorizations for their cloud products.

The Rules in Plain English

What government customers expect from you.

RequirementWhat it meansWho it affects
Federal Acquisition Regulation (FAR) 52.204-21Fifteen basic safeguards for federal contract information, such as access control, passwords, and malware protection.Most federal contractors that handle non-public contract information
Defense Federal Acquisition Regulation Supplement (DFARS) 252.204-7012Protect controlled unclassified information (CUI) using NIST SP 800-171 and report cyber incidents to the DoD within 72 hours.Defense contractors and subcontractors handling CUI
NIST SP 800-171The National Institute of Standards and Technology's 110 security requirements (Rev. 2) for protecting CUI. You score yourself and submit the score to the Supplier Performance Risk System (SPRS).Defense contractors handling CUI
Cybersecurity Maturity Model Certification (CMMC)The DoD program that verifies contractors actually follow these requirements, through self-assessments or third-party assessments depending on the level. Phase 2 is currently paused; see Regulatory Watch for the latest.Defense contractors and subcontractors
FedRAMP and GovRAMPSecurity authorizations for cloud services sold to federal agencies (FedRAMP) or state and local governments (GovRAMP).Cloud and SaaS providers

General information, not legal advice. Your contract clauses control what applies to you.

How We Help

Government contracting services.

01

Requirements scoping

Read your contract clauses with you, identify where federal contract information and CUI live, and shrink the scope that has to be protected.

02

CMMC and NIST SP 800-171 readiness

Gap assessment, a realistic SPRS score, and a prioritized plan with owners and dates.

03

System Security Plan and action plan

Write or update your System Security Plan (SSP) and Plan of Action and Milestones (POA&M) in language assessors and your team both understand.

04

Incident reporting readiness

Prepare for 72-hour DoD reporting: the right certificate, contacts, evidence preservation, and a rehearsal using our defense supplier tabletop scenario.

05

Policies, training, and flow-down

Plain-English policies, staff training, and help passing requirements down to your own subcontractors and vendors.

06

Fractional security leadership

A named virtual chief information security officer (vCISO) who answers prime-contractor questionnaires, supports proposals, and keeps you assessment-ready.

Nexus prepares you for assessment. Certifications are issued only by authorized third-party assessors and government bodies.
