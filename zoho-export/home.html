Questions

Frequently asked questions

Can Nexus act as our CISO with US customers?

Yes. As your fractional CISO, we can be the named security leader on security reviews, questionnaires, and customer calls, and we report to your founders and board. We are a U.S.-based firm working in U.S. business hours.

We already have ISO/IEC 27001. Do US buyers still want SOC 2?

Often, yes. Many U.S. enterprise buyers ask for a SOC 2 Type II report specifically. The controls overlap heavily, so an existing ISO 27001 program shortens the path; we map what you have and close the gaps.

How long does SOC 2 take?

A SOC 2 Type I report reflects a single point in time and can follow a few months of readiness work. A Type II report covers an observation period, commonly three to twelve months, so it pays to start early. We help you sequence both against your sales pipeline.

Do we need a US legal entity?

That is a legal and tax decision for your attorneys and accountants. We coordinate with them so your security program, SOC 2 scope, and contracts cover the right entities, which is a common source of delays in U.S. deals.

How do you handle the time difference?

Israel is usually seven hours ahead of U.S. Eastern Time. We schedule working sessions in the U.S. morning, which is the Israeli afternoon, and keep asynchronous updates moving across the Sunday-to-Thursday Israeli work week.

Is this legal advice?

No. We provide security and compliance leadership. For questions about law, contracts, or corporate structure, we work alongside your legal counsel.

Regulatory Watch

Where the rules stand right now.

Plain-English status on the regulations our clients ask about most, with key dates, what to do next, and links to the official sources.

Reporting obligations live since Sep 11, 2026

EU Cyber Resilience Act — Regulation (EU) 2024/2847

The CRA sets mandatory cybersecurity requirements for “products with digital elements” (hardware and software) sold in the EU, across their whole lifecycle. It applies to manufacturers, importers, and distributors, including U.S. companies selling into the EU. Fines reach up to €15 million or 2.5% of worldwide annual turnover.

  • DEC 10, 2024Entered into force
  • JUN 11, 2026Rules for conformity assessment (notified) bodies apply
  • SEP 11, 2026Manufacturers must report actively exploited vulnerabilities and severe incidents: 24-hour early warning, 72-hour notification, then a final report (14 days after a fix for vulnerabilities; one month for incidents) via ENISA's Single Reporting Platform
  • DEC 11, 2027Full application: essential requirements, conformity assessment, technical documentation, and CE marking

What to do now

  • Inventory every product with digital elements you place on the EU market
  • Stand up a 24/72-hour vulnerability and incident reporting runbook
  • Publish a coordinated vulnerability disclosure policy and generate SBOMs
  • Map secure-by-design and update obligations into your product lifecycle
  • Plan conformity assessment and CE marking for the December 2027 deadline

Phase 2 suspended — under review

CMMC 2.0: where things stand

On July 13, 2026, the Department of War paused Phase 2, which would have made third-party (C3PAO) Level 2 certification the default for many contracts starting November 10, 2026. It also launched a 60-day CMMC Reform Task Force and a public request for information. As of late October 2026, no resumption date has been announced. The 32 CFR Part 170 rule itself remains on the books.

  • DEC 16, 2024CMMC program rule (32 CFR Part 170) takes effect
  • NOV 10, 2025Phase 1 begins: Level 1 and Level 2 self-assessments in contracts (DFARS rule)
  • JUL 13, 2026Phase 2 (C3PAO by default) suspended; reform task force and RFI launched
  • SEP 2026Class deviation tells contracting officers to accept self-assessments and remove third-party assessment requirements at the next modification
  • OCT 2026Task force review closed; final determinations pending

What still applies today

  • DFARS 252.204-7012 safeguarding and 72-hour incident reporting
  • NIST SP 800-171 Rev. 2 (110 requirements) for systems handling CUI
  • Level 1 / Level 2 self-assessments, SPRS scores, and annual senior-official affirmations
  • Existing C3PAO certifications remain valid for their three-year term
  • False Claims Act exposure for inaccurate affirmations continues

Our view: the pause is a window to get audit-ready, not a reason to stop. Requirements can return quickly.

Security Rule overhaul proposed — not final

HIPAA Security Rule

The current HIPAA Security Rule remains in force. HHS proposed the first major update in two decades (published January 6, 2025). The federal regulatory agenda now lists final action no earlier than mid-2027, and agenda dates are not binding.

  • JAN 6, 2025Proposed rule published in the Federal Register
  • MAR 7, 2025Public comment period closed
  • 2026Final rule delayed; current rule still enforced by HHS OCR

What the proposal would require

  • Remove “addressable” flexibility: MFA, encryption, and segmentation become expected
  • Written asset inventory and network map for systems touching ePHI
  • Risk analysis reviewed at least every 12 months
  • Vulnerability scans every 6 months and penetration tests every 12 months
  • Ability to restore critical systems within 72 hours

Smart move: adopt HHS 405(d) Health Industry Cybersecurity Practices now; they line up with where the rule is heading.

PCI DSS v4.0.1 fully in effect

PCI DSS v4.0.1

Version 4.0.1 is the active standard for anyone who stores, processes, or transmits cardholder data. The “future-dated” requirements introduced in v4.0 became mandatory on March 31, 2025, so assessments now test all of them.

  • MAR 31, 2024PCI DSS v3.2.1 retired
  • JUN 2024v4.0.1 published (clarifications, no new requirements)
  • MAR 31, 2025Future-dated requirements became mandatory

Where teams most often fall short

  • MFA for all access into the cardholder data environment
  • Targeted risk analyses to justify control frequencies
  • Inventory and integrity monitoring of payment-page scripts (e-commerce skimming)
  • Authenticated internal vulnerability scanning
  • Stronger password and service-account management

Our Approach

Tools find the issues. People close them.

Every finding gets a plain-language title, a business impact, a named owner, a due date, and a retest date. Have the plan in place, then work the plan.

What is a fractional CISO (vCISO)?

A fractional or virtual CISO is a senior security executive who leads your security program part-time. You get CISO-level strategy, risk management, and board reporting without the cost of a full-time hire.

Who is a good fit for Nexus Security Advisors?

Small and mid-sized businesses in any industry, including financial services, healthcare, defense suppliers, manufacturing, technology, and professional services, that need executive security leadership, are facing an audit or assessment, or are rolling out AI and need it governed.

Can you help us prepare for CMMC?

Yes. We assess your current state against NIST SP 800-171 and CMMC requirements, identify gaps, and build a prioritized, owner-assigned plan to close them.

How do you approach AI governance?

We start by finding the AI tools and agents actually in use, then build AI risk into each initiative's business case, apply least privilege to AI agents, and align to frameworks such as NIST AI RMF, ISO/IEC 42001, the EU AI Act, and the OWASP AI Exchange.

Can we sign an NDA before sharing details?

Yes. We are happy to sign a mutual NDA before any detailed discussion of your environment.

Do you replace our IT team or MSP?

No. We provide security leadership and oversight and work alongside your IT team or managed service provider, helping them prioritize and measure what matters.

How is a fractional CISO engagement priced?

Usually as a predictable monthly retainer sized to your needs, or a fixed fee for project work such as an assessment or readiness sprint. We recommend an option after the free discovery call.
